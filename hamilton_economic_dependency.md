# Hamilton, Montana: Economic Dependency and Structural Conflict of Interest
## Rocky Mountain Laboratories Program Integrity Investigation
### Author: Dawn Krysa, PA-C, MSHIIM, CHDA | June 2026

---

## Overview

Hamilton, Montana (population ~5,000, Ravalli County ~43,000) is a company town.
The company is a federal BSL-4 laboratory.

This document maps the complete economic dependency network that has developed
around Rocky Mountain Laboratories over 120 years -- from the original NIH
research investment through commercial spinoffs, municipal infrastructure deals,
and healthcare system dependencies -- and analyzes what that dependency means
for independent oversight of a facility now at the center of a federal
criminal investigation.

---

## The Five Economic Pillars

### Pillar 1: Rocky Mountain Laboratories (NIH/NIAID) -- The Anchor

- **Employees:** ~450
- **Personal income generated:** $105M+ annually (University of Montana, 2023)
- **Total economic impact:** "Hundreds of millions of dollars for the local economy"
- **Federal contracts (USASpending, all years):** $737,622,338
- **Federal assistance awards (ZIP 59840):** $64,811,856
- **Status as of June 2026:** Active federal criminal investigation; senior
  virologist charged with conspiracy to smuggle monkeypox and making false
  statements to federal law enforcement

RML is the economic foundation on which everything else in Hamilton rests.
Without RML, there is no GSK Hamilton. Without GSK, there is no adjuvant
manufacturing cluster. Without that cluster, Bitterroot Health loses a
significant portion of its insured patient base. The entire local economy
is downstream of federal decisions about one laboratory.

---

### Pillar 2: GSK (GlaxoSmithKline) -- The Commercial Spinoff

- **Location:** 553 Old Corvallis Road, Hamilton, MT 59840
- **Facility:** ~130,000 sq ft manufacturing campus
- **Employees:** ~200 (manufacturing, post-R&D closure in 2015)
- **Products:**
  - Monophosphoryl Lipid A (MPL adjuvant) -- Hamilton is the **sole US
    manufacturing site** for this compound, used in multiple GSK vaccines
  - QS-21 adjuvant -- new manufacturing building opened May 2023; used
    in Shingrix (shingles vaccine), one of GSK's top revenue products
- **Facility investment:** $137M expansion (2007) + new QS-21 building (2023)
- **EPA permit:** MT-PF00103 (industrial wastewater pretreatment)

**The RML Connection:**

GSK Hamilton would not exist without RML. The full lineage:

1. **1980s:** RML researcher develops vaccine adjuvant technology at the lab
2. **1981:** Technology commercialized into Ribi ImmunoChem Research (Hamilton)
3. **~2000:** Ribi acquired by Corixa Corporation (553 Old Corvallis Road -- RML's address)
4. **~2005:** Corixa acquired by GlaxoSmithKline
5. **2007:** GSK opens $137M facility expansion -- Governor and Mayor attend ribbon cutting
6. **2015:** GSK closes Hamilton R&D, retains manufacturing
7. **2023:** New QS-21 adjuvant manufacturing building opens

The adjuvant technology now manufactured at GSK Hamilton -- generating significant
revenue for one of the world's largest pharmaceutical companies -- was developed
using federal research funding at RML. GSK's entire Hamilton presence is a
downstream commercial product of the federal laboratory next door.

**City of Hamilton's financial relationship with GSK:**

In 2011, the City of Hamilton received a $240,000 state grant (Montana Big Sky
Economic Trust Fund) to assist GSK with equipment purchases. In 2023, the City
helped GSK with water and sewer infrastructure for the new QS-21 building.
Hamilton's Mayor publicly described GSK as enabling the city "to continue to
provide other infrastructure for other organizations."

The City of Hamilton has a documented financial stake in GSK's continued
Hamilton operations. GSK's Hamilton operations depend on RML's continued
existence and credibility.

### Pillar 3: Bitterroot Health (Marcus Daly Memorial Hospital) -- The Healthcare System

- **Type:** Critical Access Hospital (CAH), nonprofit
- **Location:** 1200 Westwood Drive, Hamilton, MT 59840
- **Beds:** 24 (CAH designation)
- **CMS rating:** 4-star
- **Network:** 14 clinics throughout Bitterroot Valley
- **Providers:** 90+ medical providers

**Federal dependency:**

As a Critical Access Hospital, Bitterroot Health receives enhanced Medicare
reimbursement at 101% of allowable costs (vs standard ~94%). This designation
exists because Hamilton is classified as a rural underserved area. Medicare and
Medicaid are the primary payers for a significant portion of the patient base.

**The RML/GSK connection:**

RML employs ~450 people. GSK employs ~200 people. Combined, these two employers
represent ~650 workers -- plus families -- in a county of 43,000. In a community
where 1 in 8 residents lives below the federal poverty line, the insured employee
base from RML and GSK represents a disproportionately significant revenue stream
for the local hospital system.

Bitterroot Health's financial health is indirectly tied to RML and GSK employment
levels. Mass layoffs at either institution -- which would follow serious
biosafety enforcement action -- would meaningfully affect Bitterroot Health's
patient volume and payer mix.

The City of Hamilton simultaneously:
- Provides municipal services to RML (water, sewer, roads, emergency response)
- Has local regulatory relationships with the campus
- Received federal contract money under the CARES Act vivarium project
- Facilitated GSK's expansion through infrastructure investment
- Is financially dependent on the tax base generated by RML and GSK employees

Hamilton City Council has publicly stated they view RML as an economic
necessity, not a partisan issue. In April 2025, hundreds of Hamilton residents
protested federal cuts to RML funding. The community's economic self-interest
and its oversight responsibilities point in opposite directions.

## The Structural Conflict of Interest

This is not a conspiracy. No individual institution or person is accused of
wrongdoing through their economic relationships with RML. Foreign national
researchers, local businesses, municipal governments, and healthcare systems
all have legal and legitimate economic ties to the laboratory.

**But structural conflicts of interest don't require bad actors. They require
only that every institution with oversight authority also has a financial
stake in the outcome of that oversight.**

In Hamilton, Montana:

**The City of Hamilton** -- which has code enforcement, emergency response,
and infrastructure oversight relationships with RML -- is also an RML
contractor and a GSK infrastructure partner. It has received federal money
under the same CARES Act project we have flagged for potential false
certification. It has no independent financial incentive to raise biosafety
concerns that might jeopardize federal investment in the lab.

**GSK** -- whose entire Hamilton manufacturing operation is a commercial
downstream product of RML research -- has no incentive to call for regulatory
action that might slow RML operations or damage the credibility of the
adjuvant technology platform it manufactures.

**Bitterroot Health** -- whose insured patient base includes hundreds of RML
and GSK employees -- has no independent incentive to publicly challenge
the safety record of the area's largest employer.

**The University of Montana** -- which received $26.9M in NIH research
contracts through RML and produced the economic impact study defending
RML's importance -- has no independent incentive to support oversight
that might curtail that research relationship.

**The result:** In a town of 5,000 people built around a federal BSL-4
laboratory, every institution with any local oversight or public voice
has a documented financial stake in that laboratory's continued operation.

The only independent oversight comes from federal agencies -- the same
federal agencies (NIH/NIAID, HHS-OIG) that have been documented,
in the OIG's own January 2023 report, as failing to monitor research
in this exact institutional ecosystem.

## Conclusion

The economic dependency between Hamilton, Montana and Rocky Mountain Laboratories
is not incidental -- it is the intended result of federal investment in a rural
community over 120 years. RML was designed to be the economic anchor of the
Bitterroot Valley. It has succeeded.

But that success has created a structural environment where:

1. Every local institution has a financial interest in the lab's continuation
2. Local oversight is compromised by economic dependency
3. Federal oversight (NIH/NIAID) has been documented as inadequate
4. The community's economic survival and biosafety accountability are in direct tension

The criminal charges filed on June 2, 2026 are the foreseeable consequence of
an oversight vacuum that the community's economic structure helped create and
maintain for 12 years.

That is the program integrity finding. Not a conspiracy. A system.
