# RML Foreign National Workforce Analysis
## Program Integrity Investigation -- Phase 6
### Author: Dawn Krysa, PA-C, MSHIIM, CHDA | June 2026

---

## Overview

Rocky Mountain Laboratories employs approximately 450 people across 30 buildings
on 36 acres in Hamilton, Montana. Of the 19 senior scientists and section chiefs
listed in the NIAID Intramural Scientist & Clinician Directory (filtered Hamilton MT,
retrieved June 3, 2026), **53% are foreign nationals or dual nationals.**

In the Laboratory of Virology -- the unit that operates the BSL-4 facility and
works with Tier 1 select agents including Ebola, Marburg, monkeypox, and SARS-CoV-2
-- **80% of section chiefs are foreign nationals.**

This analysis documents the known foreign nationals at RML, their research activities,
field work history, and the program integrity questions their presence raises in the
context of the June 2, 2026 criminal charges against Vincent Munster (Dutch national)
and Claude Kwe (Cameroonian national).

## The Compliance Culture Question

Munster's statement to CBP -- "I do this all the time" -- implies a normalized pattern
of non-compliance with USDA APHIS import permit requirements (9 CFR Part 122).

The Virus Ecology Section under Munster included:
- Munster himself (Dutch, arrested)
- Claude Kwe (Cameroonian, arrested)
- Neeltje van Doremalen (Dutch, now independent PI) -- former section member
- Multiple postdoctoral fellows conducting international field work

The program integrity question: Was the non-compliant transport methodology
a personal practice of Munster's alone, or was it a section-level culture
that extended to other researchers he supervised and trained?

This question cannot be answered without:
1. FOIA for all international travel authorizations from the Virus Ecology Section
2. USDA APHIS permit records (or absence thereof) for all field trips
3. FBI SRA re-evaluation for all current RML foreign nationals
4. HHS-OIG audit of deemed export compliance for all foreign national researchers

## The Deemed Export Issue

Under US export control regulations (15 CFR Parts 730-774), the transfer of
controlled technology -- including select agent research methodology -- to a
foreign national on US soil constitutes a "deemed export" requiring a license
from the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), Department of Commerce.

Monkeypox, Ebola, Marburg, MERS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2 are controlled biological
agents. Research methodology for handling, studying, and transporting these agents
at BSL-4 is controlled technology.

With 10 foreign nationals at the senior scientist level at RML:
- Did NIH obtain BIS deemed export licenses for each?
- Were annual renewals maintained?
- Were licenses current at the time of the Munster/Kwe arrest?

This is a FOIA target: BIS, NIH Office of Technology Transfer.

## Program Integrity Framework

This analysis raises four categories of audit questions:

**1. FBI SRA Process Integrity**
Munster and Kwe both passed FBI Security Risk Assessments to obtain select agent
access. The FBI's own clearance process did not detect or flag 12 years of allegedly
non-compliant sample transport. How robust is that process for the other foreign
nationals at this facility?

**2. Deemed Export Compliance**
10 foreign nationals at senior level have access to Tier 1 select agent research
technology at a BSL-4 facility. Were deemed export licenses obtained and maintained
for each? This is not a routine FOIA -- it is a critical national security question.

**3. Compliance Culture**
The "I do this all the time" statement implies normalization. An OIG audit should
determine whether non-compliant transport was limited to Munster or extended to
others trained in his section.

**4. Institutional Oversight**
The Chief Scientist of the BSL-4 facility is a German national who came from
Canada's Public Health Agency. The majority of the Lab of Virology leadership
are foreign nationals. NIAID's oversight framework for this population -- SRA,
deemed export, behavioral monitoring -- requires immediate independent review.
